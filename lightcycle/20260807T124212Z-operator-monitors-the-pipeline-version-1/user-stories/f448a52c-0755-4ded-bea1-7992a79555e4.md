## See the hierarchy from its root node down

**Who:** Operator

**What:** Opening the tree from any node walks up to whatever its actual root is - a theme if one exists, otherwise the top-level item itself - and shows the full hierarchy down from there.

**Why:** So I always have full context, regardless of whether this work happens to sit under a theme.

**Activity:** Inspect one item's journey - Understand a specific item end-to-end - its artifacts, child steps, and logs - to see what happened or why it's stuck.

**Step:** Explore the hierarchy

**Status:** ready

### Acceptance Criteria

- Given a node under a theme, when I open the hierarchy from it, then it shows that theme at the top with all its items and their steps below.
- Given a themeless item, when I open the hierarchy from it or one of its steps, then it shows that item as the root, with its steps below - no blank or missing theme row.
- Given the hierarchy is showing, when I look at any node, then its depth (theme/item/step) is visible by indentation.

- [wireframe-hub.html#blocked-dependency - LC-72 themeless root example](../wireframes/wireframe-hub.md)

### Context

Live-store check (2026-07-23): themes are optional in the model, and 165 of 250 items (66%) have no parent theme - most real work is themeless, root-level items. The tree can't assume a theme root.

Every node already carries a `theme` field, but it isn't a shortcut for finding the root: for an item node the store derives it fresh from `parent` on every read, while for a step node it's a metadata column that ordinary step creation never populates - `file_step` files a step with only `parent` set, and the one place that touches a step's `theme` metadata (`unblock_step`) just reads it back and writes the same value, so it stays unset in the normal case. Root-detection has to walk the live `parent` chain (step to item, item to theme-or-none) rather than read `theme` off a step node, or it will silently show no theme for steps whose item genuinely has one.
