## WI-034: Hierarchy: keep ancestry pinned while scrolling

**Brief:** As the operator scrolls past a node's parent item or theme, pin that ancestor's row to the top
of the hierarchy body (as an overlay layered above the scrolling row set) instead of letting it scroll
away; un-pin it once its real row is back in normal view.

### Acceptance Criteria

Copied verbatim from the source story:

- Given I scroll down past a node's parent item or theme, when they leave the visible scroll area, then
  their row stays pinned to the top instead of scrolling away.
- Given I scroll back up to where the ancestor's actual row is, when it comes back into normal view, then
  the pinned duplicate is no longer shown.

### Dependencies

- WI-033.

### Constraints

- Pinned row uses its own distinct visual treatment (see the wireframe excerpt above), not the ordinary
  row style.
- Only the *scrolled-past-ancestor* case is specified by the wireframe (an item scrolled past its theme).
  The two-ancestors-deep case (a step scrolled past both its item and its theme) is explicitly unsettled
  - see Open Questions.

### Verification

- Pilot test: scrolling an item-rooted hierarchy past its theme row shows the theme pinned at the top.
- Pilot test: scrolling back up until the theme's real row is visible again removes the pinned duplicate.
- Pilot test (per whatever the `[designer]` question below resolves to): a step scrolled past both its
  item and theme produces the resolved behavior consistently.

### Open Questions

- `[designer]` Whether pinning stacks both ancestor rows (item + theme) once both have scrolled out of
  view for a step, or shows only the nearer one - the design material genuinely does not settle this; no
  principle or existing story resolves it either.

### Deliverable

A scrolled-past ancestor row stays visibly pinned to the top of the hierarchy and un-pins correctly when
scrolled back into view, per whatever the open `[designer]` question resolves to for the two-deep case.
