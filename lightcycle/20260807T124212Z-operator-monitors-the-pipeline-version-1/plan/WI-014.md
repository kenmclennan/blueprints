## WI-014: Scroll the list with arrow keys

**Brief:** Keyboard-only navigation through the full (all three groups) priority list: arrows move the
selection one row, Ctrl-U/Ctrl-D move roughly a screen at a time, both clamp at the ends rather than
wrapping.

### Acceptance Criteria

Copied verbatim from the source story:

- Given the list has more rows than fit on screen, when I press up/down, then the selection moves to the
  previous/next row, scrolling as needed.
- Given the selection is at the first or last row, when I press past the end, then it stays put rather
  than wrapping.
- Given the list is longer than one screen, when I press Ctrl+U or Ctrl+D, then the view jumps by roughly
  a full screen at a time.

### Dependencies

- WI-011, WI-012, WI-013 (all three groups must exist to scroll "the full list").

### Constraints

- No wrap-around at either end (explicit AC-2).
- Establishes the global up/down + Ctrl-U/Ctrl-D scroll mechanism this design reuses verbatim on the
  Hierarchy tab (WI-033), the live log buffer (WI-047), the Backlog (WI-050), and the artifact list
  (WI-040) - do not re-derive it per screen; those items should cite this one for the mechanism.

### Verification

- Pilot test: with more rows than fit on screen, arrow-down moves selection and scrolls when needed.
- Pilot test: pressing up at the first row (or down at the last) leaves the selection unchanged.
- Pilot test: Ctrl-U/Ctrl-D move the visible window by roughly a full screen.

### Open Questions

- `[designer]` Behavior once the live store holds substantially more than today's real counts (e.g.
  well beyond 250 items) is not specified anywhere in this design - no pagination, virtualization, or
  degradation behavior is named. This item builds against today's real scale only; the same gap applies
  wherever this scroll mechanism is reused (WI-033's Hierarchy tab, WI-047's live log buffer, WI-050's
  Backlog) - it is not re-raised on each of those items individually.

### Deliverable

Arrow-key and Ctrl-U/Ctrl-D navigation works correctly across the full priority list, clamped at both
ends.
