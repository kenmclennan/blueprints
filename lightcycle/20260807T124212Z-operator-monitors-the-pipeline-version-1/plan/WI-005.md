# WI-005 - Node hub and hierarchy tab

## What

## In scope

- The hub shell: header fields, tab strip, tab cycling, and contextual tab landing.
- The hierarchy tab in full: the tree from the root down, per-node id, status and role, the content indicator, the current-node highlight, sticky ancestry, keyboard scrolling, opening a node, and the two jump keys.
- Back-navigation from the hub to wherever it was opened from, restoring position - the priority list or the backlog alike.
- The read this tab needs: composing a whole hierarchy from its root down. See the note below; this is unbuilt engine work and belongs to this item.
- The depth criterion of [Switch to the backlog with a tab or shortcut](../user-stories/5fa1445f-dc04-4322-b1be-e00496e8d0d9.md), for a jump made from this screen. WI-004 owns that story's other criteria.
- Elapsed time in the header, reusing WI-002's formatting.
- AC-3 only of [See whether Claude is available to process work](../user-stories/02c20dfb-88af-4b91-887b-6c9f1fa660aa.md) - that the hub shows a reclaimed step's real state. Its other criteria are WI-003's.

## Notes from the codebase

**The hierarchy read does not exist yet.** `TraceUseCase` (`lightcycle/application/work/trace.py`) returns one item plus its direct step children - `self._store.children(input.item)`, a single level. The hierarchy this tab renders runs from a root node down through items to their steps, so composing it is real unbuilt work in `application/`, not a call to an existing use case. It is the read-model gap the architecture's Change 4 anticipates, and it is the largest unknown in this item - larger than the widget question below.

What does exist: `TraceNode` already carries `role`, so that API-contract gap is genuinely closed and locked by a feature test. The content indicator's underlying data is in place too - `Artifact` carries `internal` and `kind`, so a non-internal count needs nothing inferred at read time.

The elapsed formatter WI-002 builds is reused for the header rather than written again.

**Second risk:** the architecture selects Textual's `Tree` widget for this tab on the basis that always-expanded, sticky-ancestry rendering is close to its out-of-the-box behaviour. Sticky ancestry and the custom row rendering are the unproven part of this design, and the same class of mismatch - a widget's native rendering fighting a specified layout - is what produced the fidelity failure this plan was rebuilt after. If `Tree` cannot honour the wireframe, that is an open question, not a licence to render something close.

Where the design and the codebase disagree, the design wins.
